At breakfast, your four-year-old reaches for her toast and you gently say, "What do we say first?" She grins and says, "Bismillah!" It is a tiny moment, but it is exactly how a Muslim child's heart gets trained: not by one big lesson, but by hundreds of small, loving reminders through the day.

This guide gives you **12 daily duas for kids**: short, authentic supplications from the Quran and Sunnah, with meanings and simple ways to teach them without pressure. It is part of our hub guide [Islamic Parenting: The Complete Guide](islamic-parenting-guide.html).

*A note on accuracy:* below we give transliteration and meaning, with the source. For exact Arabic pronunciation, learn from a teacher or a trusted printed source and check any Arabic text before putting it on posters or printables.

## Why Teach Duas Early?

The Prophet ﷺ said, "Du'a is worship" (Sunan at-Tirmidhi, graded hasan sahih). When children learn to talk to Allah in daily moments, they learn that He is near and that every part of life is connected to Him. The Prophet ﷺ taught the young Umar ibn Abi Salamah to say Bismillah when eating (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim). It is never too early to start.

## The 12 Daily Duas

**1. Waking up**
*Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur.*
"All praise is for Allah who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the resurrection." (Sahih al-Bukhari)
*Kid-friendly:* "Thank You Allah for a new day!"

**2. Before eating**
*Bismillah.* "In the name of Allah." (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim)
If you forget at the start: *Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirahu*, "In the name of Allah at its beginning and its end." (Sunan Abi Dawud and Sunan at-Tirmidhi, graded hasan)

**3. After eating**
*Alhamdu lillahil-ladhi at'amani hadha wa razaqanihi min ghayri hawlin minni wa la quwwah.*
"All praise is for Allah who fed me this and provided it for me without any power or strength from me." (Sunan Abi Dawud and Sunan at-Tirmidhi, graded hasan)
*Kid-friendly:* "Alhamdulillah for my yummy food!"

**4. Entering the bathroom**
*Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-khubuthi wal-khaba'ith.*
"O Allah, I seek refuge in You from evil and evil ones." (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim)

**5. Leaving home**
*Bismillah, tawakkaltu 'alallah, wa la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah.*
"In the name of Allah, I place my trust in Allah, and there is no might or power except with Allah." (Sunan Abi Dawud and Sunan at-Tirmidhi, graded hasan)

**6. Getting into a car or travelling**
*Subhanal-ladhi sakh-khara lana hadha wa ma kunna lahu muqrinin, wa inna ila rabbina lamunqalibun.*
"Glory be to the One who has subjected this to us, and we could never have done it ourselves, and to our Lord we will return." (Quran 43:13–14; the Prophet ﷺ recited this when mounting his animal, Sahih Muslim)

**7. Sneezing**
After a sneeze say *Alhamdulillah*. The one who hears says *Yarhamukallah* ("May Allah have mercy on you"), and the sneezer replies *Yahdikumullah wa yuslihu balakum* ("May Allah guide you and set right your affairs"). (Sahih al-Bukhari)

**8. When you are upset or worried**
*Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal-wakil.* "Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs." (Quran 3:173)

**9. When you learn something**
*Rabbi zidni 'ilma.* "My Lord, increase me in knowledge." (Quran 20:114)

**10. For your parents**
*Rabbir-hamhuma kama rabbayani saghira.* "My Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me when I was small." (Quran 17:24)

**11. Before sleeping**
*Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya.* "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live." (Sahih al-Bukhari)
The Prophet ﷺ also recited the last three surahs of the Quran (Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas) and wiped over his body before sleep (Sahih al-Bukhari), and Ayat al-Kursi was taught as protection through the night (Sahih al-Bukhari).

**12. A du'a for the heart**
*Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanah wa fil-akhirati hasanah wa qina 'adhaban-nar.*
"Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire." (Quran 2:201)

## How to Teach Duas Without Pressure

**Pick one at a time.** Choose one du'a for a week, such as Bismillah before eating, and ignore the rest. The "one habit at a time" rule wins.

**Link it to a trigger.** Each du'a is tied to a moment: the spoon, the shoes, the pillow. The moment *is* the reminder.

**Say it with them.** Children copy what they hear daily. Say it aloud, slowly and warmly.

**Use meaning.** Explain in one sentence: "We say Bismillah because Allah gave us this food."

**Make it visible.** A small picture on the fridge or by the front door helps. Use our [printables](printables.html) or make your own.

**Sing it.** Short rhymes and melodies are very effective for memory.

**Praise effort, not accuracy.** "I loved hearing your du'a today!"

<div class="tip"><b>Try this tonight:</b> After dinner, ask, "What do we say after eating?" and say it together. Do this every night for a week.</div>

## Duas by Age

| Age | How to teach |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Say them aloud; let them echo "Bismillah" and "Alhamdulillah" |
| 4–6 | Add one du'a per week; use pictures, songs and routines |
| 7–9 | Teach meanings; let them lead du'a at meals or before travel |
| 10+ | Encourage personal du'a in their own words, plus the Prophetic ones |

## Make Du'a for Your Children

The Prophet ﷺ said that the du'a of a parent for their child is among the du'as that are answered (Sunan at-Tirmidhi and Sunan Abi Dawud, graded hasan). Make your own du'a for them by name, especially in sujood and at night. And teach them that they can ask Allah for anything, in any language.

## Common Mistakes

- **Teaching too many at once.** One at a time.
- **Turning it into a test.** Gentle reminders beat quizzes.
- **Skipping the meaning.** Understanding brings love.
- **Forgetting your own example.** Say them yourself.
- **Putting wrong Arabic on printables.** Check text with a teacher or a reliable source.

## Your One Step This Week

Choose *one* du'a (Bismillah before meals is perfect) and say it with your child at every meal this week.
